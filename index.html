Why these numbers matter

Most desks are built for an “average” person that doesn’t exist. If your desk is too high you shrug your shoulders for hours; too low and you hunch your spine. Four measurements fix almost everything:

  • Desk at elbow height — forearms rest level, wrists stay neutral while typing.
  • Chair supporting the posture — seat height lets both feet rest flat.
  • Monitor top at eye level — no forward-head tilt, less neck strain.
  • Frequent position changes — the best posture is the next one.

Frequently asked questions

What is the ideal desk height for my height?

Sitting: about 40–43% of your height in cm (a 175 cm person → ~70–75 cm). Standing: about 63–68% (~110–119 cm). Use the calculator above for your exact range.

Is a standard 75 cm desk too high for me?

Under ~180 cm of body height it usually is, at least for typing. Fixes: raise the chair and add a footrest, use a keyboard tray, or pick a desk with a lower fixed height.

How often should I switch between sitting and standing?

Frequently and briefly beats rarely and long. A practical pattern: change every 30–60 minutes and move during calls. Don’t stand motionless for hours.

Do standing desks help with back pain?

Honestly: evidence is mixed. They cut sitting time and many users feel better, but regular movement breaks matter more than furniture. This site isn’t medical advice — see a professional for persistent pain.
