How to Choose a Standing Desk (2026 Buyer’s Framework)

Updated August 2026 · 7 min read · Free tool: height calculator

Spec sheets are designed to confuse. This page gives you five checks that separate desks that last from desks that wobble — applicable to any brand, any price, including secondhand listings.

1. First, know your required height range

Before comparing anything, run your numbers in our calculator. Your standing desk height is ~63–68% of your body height; add 10 cm headroom for shoe soles and elbow angles. A desk whose max height barely reaches your minimum is a non-starter regardless of price. Tall users (190+ cm) and short users (<160 cm) get burned by ranges more often than anyone.

2. Motors: one vs. two

  • Single motor: cheaper, slower, struggles near load limits. Fine for light setups changed occasionally.
  • Dual motor: faster travel, better stability under weight, quieter under load. The default recommendation for daily sit–stand use.
  • Manual crank / pneumatic: zero electronics to fail and genuinely fine for occasional position changes — an underrated budget option if you won’t switch many times a day.

3. Stability beats specs

Marketing lists weight capacity; real-world misery comes from wobble. In a showroom or via return-friendly sellers, test: grab the desktop edges at full extension and push diagonally. Some flex is normal; visible shaking of your monitor is not. Two structural factors drive it:

  • Crossbar vs. C-legs: a crossbar between the legs adds rigidity; pure C-frame legs trade stability for legroom.
  • Foot depth: longer feet = less tipping forward/back.

4. What each price tier realistically buys

TierWhat you typically getWatch out for
Budget (~$150–300)Single motor or crank, adequate for one monitor + laptopSagging desktops <18 mm thick, vague “50 kg” ratings
Mid ($300–600)Dual motors, memory presets, solid warrantiesPreset heights calibrated for nobody — reprogram them
Premium ($600+)Better frames, finishes, longer warrantiesYou’re partly paying for looks; mid-tier often measures equally stable

Well-established brands across these tiers include IKEA’s motorized options, FlexiSpot, Autonomous, Uplift and Vari — compare specific current models on the checks above rather than trusting any single review score.

5. Warranty and service red flags

  • Lift mechanism warranty under 5 years on a dual-motor desk — the motors are what fail.
  • No spare-parts policy (control boxes and handsets die before legs do).
  • Reviews mentioning slow or unresponsive support matter more than review scores about delivery speed.

Quick decision shortcut

Switch positions a few times a day and keep the desk light → manual or single motor saves you money. Daily multi-session sit–stand work → dual motor, memory presets, ≥5-year lift warranty, and a frame that passes the push test at your height range.
